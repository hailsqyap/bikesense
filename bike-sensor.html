BikeSense Recorder

Microphone sound features + accelerometer + gyroscope + GPS, logged to CSV. Raw audio is never stored.

This page is not running in a secure context (https). Phones block the microphone and motion sensors on plain http. Host it on HTTPS (for example GitHub Pages or Netlify Drop) and open that link on the phone.

1 · Session setup

How to get good data
  • Keep the screen on and this tab in front. Phones pause sensors when the screen locks or you switch apps. The page asks the phone to stay awake, but turn off auto-lock to be safe.
  • Mount rigidly. A wobbly mount looks like road bumps. The handlebar phone turns when you steer, so the frame phone is the better source for braking and accelerating; the handlebar phone also picks up your steering.
  • Wind is the biggest sound problem. At more than about 15 km/h, wind on the mic sounds loud and low-pitched. A small foam windscreen or a sock over the mic end helps a lot. Watch the "wind / low-freq" label.
  • Calibrate sound once. Place the phone next to a sound level meter (or a calibrated meter app) in steady noise, then enter the difference as the offset.
  • Ground truth: have a teammate film the ride with a third phone or action camera, or use the marker buttons at stops. You'll need it to check whether "voices-like" sound really means a crowd.
  • Privacy: only sound features (levels, frequency balance, rhythm) are saved, once per second. No audio is recorded, so speech can't be played back. GPS can still reveal where riders live, so start and stop recording away from homes.
